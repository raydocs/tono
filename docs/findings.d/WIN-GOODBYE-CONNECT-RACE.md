| WIN-GOODBYE-CONNECT-RACE | Accepted idle owner-goodbye leaves lifecycle admission open during its 250 ms response grace, so shutdown can stop newly admitted work | fixed(945bf1fa) | [#955](https://github.com/raydocs/tono/pull/955) | 低·已确认（P2） | Narrow actual-admission regression passed; requires concurrent quit/new lifecycle work in a short window; Windows hardware validation pending |

Initially recorded open in #873 while P1 fixes took priority. Baseline `b341164b`: `core/server/mod.rs:392-398` schedules shutdown without reserving it, and `handlers.rs:1028` releases the owner lifecycle guard after acceptance. #792 fences work once teardown starts, but that is after this grace period and its listener-start reset clears that flag.

Ordinary trigger: begin an update while disconnected, then Quit during its download. `tono/commands/update.rs:131,166-192` holds only INSTALL and sets INCOMPLETE immediately before Prepare; Quit can already have passed its earlier incomplete check (`commands/quit.rs:271`). The authenticated Prepare then queues behind goodbye and is admitted after its lock drops while the App is still exiting (`feat/window.rs:536`). The single-instance guard does not serialize these same-App tasks.

The reservation now sets synchronously while the accepted-goodbye route still holds `OWNER_LIFECYCLE_LOCK`. A separate process-lifetime flag survives listener restarts. Normal lifecycle admission and the update route both reject through the existing service-stopping response. Actual teardown bypasses admission, so it cannot block itself. Existing wanted/desired-state refusal remains the authority for accepting goodbye; strict/armed sessions never reserve this stop.

Regression `accepted_goodbye_refuses_new_lifecycle_work_before_its_response_grace` runs the actual admission gate immediately after accepted-idle scheduling, captures reservation before the lock drops without yielding to the grace task, and expects service unavailable. Before: a new lifecycle operation was admitted. After: the owner suites pass 16 tests. No real-machine concurrent quit/connect or native WFP test is claimed.
